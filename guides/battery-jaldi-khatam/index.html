Battery drains too fast

If you left home with a full charge in the morning and the battery was already low by noon, this guide is for you. The usual culprits are screen brightness, background apps, or an aging battery — try these steps.

Steps

  1. Lower the screen brightness
    A bright screen is the battery's biggest enemy. Open Settings > Display > Brightness and keep brightness below 50%, or turn on Adaptive Brightness.
  2. Check battery usage
    Go to Settings > Battery > Battery Usage. If an app you barely use is eating the most battery, uninstall it or turn off its background activity.
  3. Keep wireless features off when not needed
    Turn on Bluetooth, Wi-Fi hotspot and Location only when you need them. In Settings > Location, set the location permission to 'While Using' or 'Never' for apps that shouldn't have it.
  4. Set screen timeout to 30 seconds
    Set Settings > Display > Screen Timeout to 30 seconds. A screen that never turns off drains the battery fast.
  5. Use Battery Saver
    Turn on Battery Saver / Power Saving mode during the day (from the Quick Settings shade). With heavy use it can buy you 1–2 extra hours of battery.
  6. Old battery? Check its health
    If your phone is 2–3 years old, its battery capacity may have dropped. Mobile Ustaad tip: get a battery health test at an authorized service center — avoid cheap roadside batteries.

Tips

  • Social media apps running in the background eat the most battery — turn off their auto-start permissions.
  • Leaving the phone plugged in after it reaches 100% is not good for battery health.
  • Buy a certified-brand power bank instead of a cheap one — a fake power bank damages the battery.

Why a battery suddenly starts draining faster

Sometimes the battery was fine last month and now it isn't. That usually means something changed — an update, a new app, or the environment around the phone:

  • Weak mobile signal: In areas with poor coverage the phone constantly boosts its antenna power to stay connected. If your area has weak signals, this alone can quietly eat a big share of the battery.
  • Heat: Summer heat is hard on phone batteries. A phone left in a car, in direct sunlight, or used for long gaming or video sessions in a hot room drains faster, and its battery wears out sooner.
  • A recent software update: After a big system update the phone re-indexes files and re-optimises apps for a day or two. Extra drain during the first 24–48 hours is normal; it should settle on its own.
  • High refresh rate and always-on display: A 120Hz screen and an always-on clock look great but keep the display sipping power all day. Drop to 60Hz/Auto and turn off always-on display if you need the extra hours.

Hidden settings that drain quietly

  1. Turn off constant scanning
    Even with Wi-Fi off, the phone may keep scanning for networks. Go to Settings > Location > Location Services (or Wi-Fi and Bluetooth scanning) and turn off 'Wi-Fi scanning' and 'Bluetooth scanning' if you don't use location-based reminders.
  2. Prefer 4G where 5G is weak
    In patches with poor 5G coverage the phone keeps hopping between networks. In Settings > Mobile Network > Preferred network type, choose 4G/LTE preferred and the drain often drops noticeably.
  3. Stop apps waking the screen
    Frequent notifications light up the whole screen all day. In Settings > Notifications, mute the noisy, unimportant apps and keep alerts only for what matters.
  4. Restrict the worst offenders
    Back in Settings > Battery > Battery Usage, open the top-draining apps and set their background usage to 'Restricted' if you don't need their instant updates.

Habits that keep the battery healthy

  • Try to keep the charge roughly between 20% and 80% in daily use — regularly running it flat or holding it at 100% for hours wears a battery out faster.
  • Always use the charger and cable that came with the phone, or a proper certified one. Cheap chargers cause heat, and heat is the battery's worst enemy.
  • Avoid heavy gaming or video recording while charging — the phone heats up faster and the battery ages quickly.
  • If the back of the phone ever bulges or the screen starts lifting, stop using it and go to a service center — a swollen battery is a safety risk, not just a drain problem.
Warning: If nothing improves, don't open up the battery yourself, especially glued backs — that's a job for the service center.

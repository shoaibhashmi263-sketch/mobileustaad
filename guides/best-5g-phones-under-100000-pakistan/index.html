Best 5G Phones Under Rs 100,000 in Pakistan (2026)

Pakistan's 5G rollout is still in its early stages, so be honest with yourself about why you are buying a 5G phone in 2026: for most buyers it is future-proofing — a phone bought today should not feel obsolete when coverage widens. The good news is that 5G no longer costs flagship money. The cheapest sensible 5G phone in our catalog is under Rs 30,000, and every phone below also works perfectly well as a 4G phone today.

Prices are checked 18 November 2026 and labelled official or market. Imported models carry the usual warning: confirm PTA approval before paying — our PTA approval guide covers the checks.

Infinix Hot 60i 5G — 5G stops being a luxury

The Hot 60i 5G (Rs 29,999, market price) is built around the Dimensity 6400, an efficient 6nm processor with 5G, and surrounds it with the things budget buyers actually feel: a 6,000mAh battery, 128GB of storage, a 50MP triple camera and a 6.75-inch 120Hz screen. As an everyday phone it has no serious gap; as a 5G phone it is the cheapest one we can recommend.

Charging is 18W — slow against that big battery — and the selfie camera is 5MP. Those are the prices of the price.

  • Walk past it if: fast charging matters more than the 5G badge.
  • Look instead at: the Infinix Hot 60 5G for a stronger processor, though it charges no faster.

Samsung Galaxy A06 5G — the support pick

What the Galaxy A06 5G (Rs 30,700, market price) sells is not speed — its Dimensity 6300, 4GB of RAM and HD+ 90Hz screen are entry-level — it is time. Our catalog lists four OS updates and four years of security updates for it, a support promise no other phone near this price makes. Add a 50MP main camera and a 5,000mAh battery with 25W charging, and it is the 5G phone for someone who keeps a phone for years.

  • Walk past it if: you want storage and speed now more than updates later — it has 64GB and modest power.
  • Look instead at: the Infinix Hot 60 5G.

Infinix Hot 60 5G — the sensible step up

Spend about five thousand more and the Hot 60 5G (Rs 35,310, market price) upgrades the formula: a Dimensity 7020 processor with more headroom, 6GB of RAM, 128GB of storage, a 5,200mAh battery and a 6.7-inch 120Hz display. It feels like the Hot 60i's formula with the performance ceiling raised — the right pick if the phone will also do some gaming.

Charging stays at 18W and the cameras (50MP rear, 8MP front) are standard budget fare. The money went into the processor and memory, which is where a step-up phone should spend it.

  • Walk past it if: your budget is firm at Rs 30,000 — the Hot 60i 5G covers the basics for less.
  • Step down to: the Infinix Hot 60i 5G.

Lenovo Legion Y70 — a flagship chip for Rs 43,500

The Legion Y70 (Rs 43,500, market price) does not belong in this price band on paper: a Snapdragon 8 Gen 5, a 6.82-inch 2K LTPO OLED screen at 144Hz, 12GB of RAM, 256GB of storage, an 8,000mAh battery and 90W charging. It is a gaming flagship that market pricing has dragged into mid-range territory, and for performance per rupee nothing else in this guide is close.

The catch: it is an imported phone, not an official Pakistan launch. Warranty will be the seller's, and PTA approval must be verified before you buy — do not skip that step at this price.
  • Walk past it if: you want an official warranty and zero import risk.
  • Look instead at: the Infinix Note 50s 5G.

Infinix Note 50s 5G — the balanced all-rounder

The Note 50s 5G (Rs 54,999, Infinix Pakistan launch price) is what a mid-range phone should look like in 2026: a 6.78-inch AMOLED display at 144Hz, a Dimensity 7300 Ultimate processor, 8GB of RAM and 128GB of storage, a 64MP main camera, a 5,500mAh battery with 45W charging and an in-display fingerprint reader. No single spec leads this guide; the combination has no weak point.

  • Walk past it if: raw gaming power is the priority — the Legion Y70 above is far stronger for less money, import caveats and all.
  • Look instead at: the Infinix Zero 30 5G if cameras lead your list.

Infinix Zero 30 5G — the ceiling pick, built around cameras

At Rs 99,999 (official price), the Zero 30 5G spends the full budget on imaging and display: a 108MP main camera with optical image stabilisation, a 13MP ultrawide, a 50MP front camera — the strongest selfie hardware in this guide — and a curved 6.78-inch AMOLED screen at 144Hz, with 12GB of RAM, 256GB of storage, a Dimensity 8020 processor and 68W charging for its 5,000mAh battery.

It is an older model than the Note 50s, so its remaining software runway is shorter. Buy it for the cameras and screen, knowing that trade.

  • Walk past it if: you keep phones for four-plus years and update support matters most.
  • Look instead at: the Infinix Note 50s 5G and keep the change.

Do you actually need 5G today?

For most Pakistani buyers in 2026, the honest answer is: not yet — but a phone is a three-to-five-year purchase, and the 5G premium at the budget end has nearly vanished. If two phones cost the same and one has 5G, take the 5G one. If 5G costs you a better screen, camera or battery at the same money, take the better phone; our best phones under Rs 50,000 guide covers the strongest 4G alternatives. Either way, buy PTA approved, and if you are stretching the budget on installments, read the terms before signing — the phone should outlast the payments, not the other way round.

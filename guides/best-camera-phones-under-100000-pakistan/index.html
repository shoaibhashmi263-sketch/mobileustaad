Best Camera Phones Under Rs 100,000 in Pakistan

How this ranking was built — and what it is not

Camera rankings are easy to fake with a megapixel chart, so this one uses a stricter set of facts. Each phone below was ordered on its camera hardware as recorded in verified specifications: whether the main camera has optical image stabilisation (OIS), whether there is a real telephoto lens with optical zoom rather than a cropped digital substitute, whether an ultrawide is present, and what class the main sensor belongs to.

That also means being upfront about the limit of the exercise. This is a specification-based ranking, not a set of hands-on photo tests — no phone here was put through a shooting session for this guide, and megapixel counts on their own were not treated as evidence of quality. A large number next to "MP" tells you about resolution. It tells you nothing about stability, flexibility, or the sensor doing the work.

1. Tecno Camon 50 Pro — three real lenses, which nobody else here offers

The Camon 50 Pro stands apart on a simple structural point: it is the only phone in this list with a true three-lens rear system in which every lens earns its space. The main camera is a 50MP Sony LYT-700C with OIS. Beside it sits a 50MP telephoto offering 3x optical zoom — genuine glass doing the zooming, not a crop pretending to — and an 8MP ultrawide completes the set. A 32MP front camera handles selfies.

Price needs a careful sentence here. Rs 85,999 was Tecno's official Pakistan launch price in April 2026 (checked again 6 November 2026), but dealer quotes vary and some sellers ask for more, so treat any quote above that figure as negotiable rather than official.

Away from the cameras you get a 6.78-inch AMOLED display at 144Hz and a 6,500mAh battery with 45W charging — strong supporting numbers. The compromises live elsewhere: the Helio G200 chipset is a mid-range 4G processor, and there is no 5G on this phone at all.

  • The compromise: you are paying for camera versatility and accepting an ordinary chipset and 4G-only connectivity in return.
  • Pass on it if: your money is really meant for all-round performance or future-proof networking, and the camera is secondary.

2. Redmi Note 14 Pro 4G — the highest-resolution stabilised main camera in the group

Xiaomi took a different route with the Redmi Note 14 Pro 4G. Instead of spreading the budget across three lenses, it concentrated on one: a 200MP main camera at f/1.7, fitted with OIS. That pairing — the highest resolution on this page plus optical stabilisation — is its whole argument, and it is a strong one for detail-heavy daylight photography and for cropping into a shot afterwards. An 8MP ultrawide and a 2MP macro fill in around it, with a 32MP front camera.

On price, the official figure is Rs 85,999 from MiStore.pk (checked 6 November 2026), while market listings have been tracked lower, at Rs 76,499 — worth checking both before you buy. The rest of the spec sheet reads well: Helio G100 Ultra, a 5,500mAh battery with 45W charging, and a 6.67-inch AMOLED screen at 120Hz.

  • The compromise: there is no telephoto lens. Zoom beyond the main camera's reach is cropped or digital, so distant subjects are this phone's weak direction.
  • Pass on it if: you photograph sports, stage events, wildlife, or anything else you cannot walk closer to.

3. Infinix Note 50 Pro — stabilised, fast, and generously specced for the money

The Infinix Note 50 Pro covers the camera essentials without reaching for a headline number. Its 50MP main camera has OIS, an 8MP ultrawide sits alongside it, and the front camera is 32MP. That is the correct checklist for most people's actual photography: a steady main lens for everyday and low-light shots, and a wider option for groups and interiors.

It is listed at Rs 89,999, with market prices tracked at Rs 82,999 (checked 6 November 2026). Around the cameras, Infinix is generous: 12GB of RAM and 256GB of storage, a 6.78-inch AMOLED display at 144Hz, and a 5,200mAh battery with 90W charging — the fastest charging anywhere in this guide, which matters more to camera users than it first appears, because shooting drains batteries quickly.

  • The compromise: no telephoto, and the main sensor does not carry a named flagship-class sensor of the kind the Camon 50 Pro's Sony unit represents — a class below on paper.
  • Pass on it if: sensor pedigree and optical zoom sit at the top of your list; the Tecno above is built for you.

4. Tecno Camon 40 Pro — the selfie camera is the story here

Most phones in this bracket spend their front-camera budget last. The Camon 40 Pro did the opposite. Its front camera is 50MP — the highest front resolution in this guide — which makes it the natural recommendation for video callers, vloggers and anyone whose camera roll faces towards them as often as away.

The rear system remains respectable rather than stripped down: a 50MP main camera with OIS and an 8MP ultrawide. Pricing is Rs 75,999, with market listings tracked at Rs 73,999 (checked 6 November 2026), making this the most affordable entry on the page. The supporting hardware is a Helio G100 Ultimate processor, a 5,200mAh battery with 45W charging, and a 6.78-inch AMOLED display at 120Hz.

  • The compromise: again no telephoto, and the main sensor is a step down in class from its Camon 50 Pro sibling.
  • Pass on it if: selfies are an afterthought for you — in that case the same money buys a stronger rear-camera system elsewhere on this page.

5. Realme 13 Pro — a proper Sony sensor, and the only 5G phone here

The Realme 13 Pro, listed at Rs 89,999 (checked 6 November 2026), builds its camera case on sensor quality. The main camera is a 50MP Sony LYT-600 with OIS, backed by an 8MP ultrawide and a 32MP front camera. On specification alone, that Sony-plus-stabilisation combination puts its main camera among the most credible in this group.

Then there is the point that has nothing to do with photography and may decide the purchase anyway: the Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 inside makes this the only 5G phone in the list, and the strongest all-rounder of the five. If the phone must serve as a fast, long-lived daily device as well as a camera, that counts.

  • The compromise: storage. You get 128GB at a price where rivals on this page hand you 256GB — a real limitation for anyone shooting lots of high-resolution photos and video.
  • Pass on it if: you rarely delete anything and expect two or three years of photos to live on the phone itself.

Before you decide: two pieces of reasoning worth more than any badge

  1. Weigh stabilisation above raw megapixels. OIS physically steadies the lens, which helps exactly where phone photos usually fail — indoors, in the evening, in a moving car, in unsteady hands. A stabilised 50MP main camera therefore has a practical advantage over a hypothetical 200MP badge with no OIS behind it. Note that every phone above does include OIS; the point is how to judge the next phone someone tries to sell you, not just these five.
  2. Decide your zoom needs before you shop, not after. Only the Camon 50 Pro offers optical telephoto zoom here. Cropped and digital zoom can frame a distant subject, but it discards resolution to do it. Buyers who regularly shoot from a distance should start at the top of this list; everyone else can safely save the money.

Best Gaming Phones in Pakistan (2026)

Picking a gaming phone in Pakistan is mostly a chipset decision. Marketing leans hard on refresh rates and battery numbers, but those come second. Get the processor class right for your budget and most other things fall into place; get it wrong and no amount of screen smoothness will rescue a session that stutters in a team fight.

What actually decides how a game runs

The chipset does the heavy lifting. Its CPU and graphics processor decide which settings a game can hold and whether high frame-rate modes are even offered. Cooling comes next, because a phone that cannot shed heat will slow itself down partway through a long match. Only after those two does the display matter: a 120Hz or 144Hz panel can show more frames, but it cannot create them.

A 120Hz screen does not mean games run at 120fps. The chipset sets the frame rate; the screen only displays what the chip can produce. A fast panel on a weak processor still gives you a slow game, just on a nicer display.

Battery and charging shape the practical side. Larger cells last through longer sessions away from a socket, and fast charging shortens the break when you do plug in. For players who game daily, those numbers are worth as much attention as the processor.

Entry level: casual play, honestly labelled

Redmi Note 14 4G — Rs 57,999 (official)

The Helio G99-Ultra, a 6.67-inch AMOLED display at 120Hz and a 5,500mAh battery with 33W charging make this a sensible starting point. Expect popular titles such as PUBG Mobile and Call of Duty: Mobile to run at low-to-medium graphics settings, while lighter games run smoothly. If your gaming is occasional and social rather than competitive, this class is enough. If you chase high frame rates, it is not.

Mid-range: where high settings become realistic

Infinix GT 20 Pro — Rs 87,999

Built around the Dimensity 8200 Ultimate, a chip from a former-flagship class, the GT 20 Pro pairs it with a 6.78-inch AMOLED 144Hz screen, 5G, 12GB of RAM and 256GB of storage. Its battery is 5,000mAh with 45W charging, and it still finds room for a 108MP camera with optical image stabilisation. At this level, high settings in popular shooters and high frame-rate modes stop being a stretch. Among the phones here, it is the strongest gaming value under Rs 100,000.

Upper mid-range: two different routes to speed

Infinix Note 60 Pro — Rs 125,999 (official)

The Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 drives a 6.78-inch AMOLED display at 144Hz, backed by an unusually large 6,500mAh battery and 90W charging. That combination suits players who want solid high-settings gaming without draining the phone before the evening is over — performance and endurance in roughly equal measure.

Poco X7 Pro — Rs 139,999 (PriceOye)

Poco takes the other route and spends the money on raw power. The Dimensity 8400 Ultra is a near-flagship chip, matched with 12GB of RAM, 512GB of storage, a 6.67-inch AMOLED 120Hz display and a 6,000mAh battery with 90W charging. High settings and high frame-rate modes in supported titles are well within reach, making this the performance-per-rupee peak of this list.

Flagship class: maximum settings, maximum price

Infinix Note 60 Ultra — Rs 199,999

The Dimensity 8400 Ultimate, a 144Hz AMOLED display and the largest battery in this guide — 7,000mAh, with 100W charging — point to one priority: flagship-class play that can be sustained. If your sessions are long and you dislike mid-evening charging breaks, this is the phone shaped around that habit.

Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra — Rs 414,999 (official)

Samsung's Snapdragon 8 Elite sits in the top chipset class available, driving a 6.9-inch QHD+ LTPO AMOLED display at 120Hz, with a 5,000mAh battery and 45W charging. Everything runs at maximum settings. Be clear about what the price buys, though: you are paying for the complete flagship package — display, cameras, build and software — not for gaming alone.

Keeping a gaming phone going

  • Match the phone to the games you actually play. A flagship chip is wasted on light titles, and an entry chip will frustrate a competitive player within a week.
  • Treat storage as a gaming spec. Large games and their updates fill a phone quickly, so the 512GB Poco deserves credit for removing that worry.
  • Watch battery behaviour over time, not just on day one. If endurance starts dropping noticeably, our guide on battery draining too fast works through the likely causes.
  • Before any market-channel purchase, verify the exact unit — model, variant and PTA status — rather than relying on the shop's general description.

Best Phones for Students in Pakistan (2026)

A student's phone does a specific job: it leaves home at 8am, runs classes, notes, group chats, hotspot sharing and the bus ride home, and must still be alive at night — on a budget that competes with books, fees and food. So this list is built on three student rules: a battery of at least 5,000mAh, at least 128GB of storage unless the price is very low, and nothing above Rs 40,000.

Prices are from our catalog, checked 18 November 2026. Whatever you buy, make it a PTA-approved unit — a blocked phone is a disaster mid-semester — and check the status before paying with our PTA approval guide.

Samsung Galaxy A06 — when the budget is really Rs 25,000

The Galaxy A06 (Rs 22,499, PriceOye listing) leaves the most money in your pocket for everything else student life charges for. It still covers the rules: a 5,000mAh battery with 25W charging, a 50MP main camera, and a published update policy of two OS updates and four years of security updates — rare honesty at this price.

The compromises are 64GB of storage and a basic HD+ screen. If your use is classes, messaging, browsing and video, it is enough; if you store lots of recorded lectures, budget for a memory card or step up.

  • Walk past it if: you download lectures and media heavily.
  • Step up to: the Poco C75 and its 256GB.

Poco C75 — the lecture-and-media phone

The Poco C75 (Rs 36,999) is built around student media habits: the biggest screen in this guide at 6.88 inches with a 120Hz refresh rate, 256GB of storage for recorded lectures and downloaded courses, 8GB of RAM, and a 5,160mAh battery. The 50MP main camera and 13MP selfie camera cover class notes and group photos well.

Charging is 18W — plug in overnight rather than between classes — and the Helio G81 Ultra is an everyday processor, not a gaming one.

  • Walk past it if: fast charging between classes is your routine.
  • Look instead at: the Vivo Y28.

Itel S24 — for the student who documents everything

The Itel S24 (Rs 36,999, PriceOye listing) pairs a 108MP main camera — the sharpest here, genuinely useful for photographing whiteboards and textbook pages — with 256GB of storage and 8GB of RAM. Its 5,000mAh battery and 90Hz display complete a package aimed squarely at students who photograph and store a lot.

The Helio G91 is a budget processor and charging is 18W. As a study tool rather than a games machine, it is one of the smartest buys here.

  • Walk past it if: gaming matters more than the camera.
  • Look instead at: the Infinix Hot 50.

Vivo Y28 — the battery that survives the whole day, and the hostel night

The Vivo Y28 (Rs 38,999, PriceOye listing) leads this guide on endurance: a 6,000mAh battery with 44W charging — the fastest here — so a short hostel-socket window actually counts. It adds 8GB of RAM, 128GB of storage and a 50MP main camera.

The HD+ screen is its weak point next to the Poco's bigger panel. If your day is long and outlets are scarce, this is the safest pick on the page.

  • Walk past it if: you watch a lot of video and want the biggest, smoothest screen.
  • Look instead at: the Poco C75.

Tecno Spark 40C — big battery, current model, official support

The Spark 40C (Rs 39,999, Tecno Pakistan price) matches the Y28's 6,000mAh battery in a newer model running Android 15, with a 6.67-inch 120Hz display, 4GB of RAM and 128GB of storage — and Tecno's official Pakistan network behind it, which matters when a student phone breaks far from home.

Charging is 18W and the rear camera is a basic 13MP unit. Buy it for battery, screen smoothness and support, not photography.

  • Walk past it if: you photograph notes and boards daily.
  • Look instead at: the Itel S24.

Infinix Hot 50 — the all-rounder for students who also game

The Infinix Hot 50 (Rs 39,999) is the most balanced phone here: a Helio G100 — the strongest processor in this guide — with 8GB of RAM and 128GB of storage, a triple camera (50MP + 8MP + 2MP), a 5,000mAh battery and a 120Hz-class everyday experience that handles study apps and casual gaming equally well.

Its battery is the joint-smallest here and charging is 18W. Students who game a little and study a lot will find it the easiest phone on this page to live with.

  • Walk past it if: your days are 14 hours away from a charger.
  • Look instead at: the Vivo Y28.

Student money rules before you buy

Three habits save students real money. First, fix before you replace: a slow old phone often needs only the steps in our slow phone guide, and a full one needs our storage guide — both free. Second, back up your notes and photos before changing phones; our WhatsApp backup guide covers the chat history students actually lose. Third, be careful with installment plans: compare the total you will repay, not the monthly figure, and never let the payments outlast the phone. Buy PTA approved, keep the warranty card in your documents file, and the phone will do its job until graduation.

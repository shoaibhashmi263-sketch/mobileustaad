Best Phones Under Rs 50,000 in Pakistan (2026)

Rs 50,000 sounds like a comfortable budget until you actually start shopping in Pakistan. At that point the shelf fills up with phones that look similar on a box — big screen, big battery, a 50-megapixel badge — and differ mostly in the things the box does not shout about: how long the software will be supported, whether the screen is AMOLED or plain LCD, and how slow the charging really is.

Every phone below is officially sold in Pakistan, and every price quoted is the official price, checked 6 November 2026. That matters more than it sounds. A grey-market unit can look a few thousand rupees cheaper on the day, then cost you far more in PTA trouble and missing warranty. Samsung Galaxy A07 — buy it for the next six years, not the next six months

Most budget phones are abandoned by their makers almost as soon as they are sold. The Galaxy A07, at Rs 45,999 official price (WhatMobile listing, checked 6 November 2026), is the odd one out: our catalog lists six years of OS updates and six years of security updates for it, which is exceptional at this price and, frankly, unmatched anywhere else on this page.

The rest of the hardware is sensible rather than exciting. A Helio G99 processor with 4GB RAM and 64GB storage handles everyday apps and light gaming without drama, the 6.7-inch PLS LCD runs at 90Hz (though only at HD+ resolution), and the 50MP + 2MP rear camera pair and 8MP selfie camera cover the basics. The 5,000mAh battery charges at 25W — not fast, not painfully slow.

  • Walk past it if: the display is what you stare at all day and you want proper AMOLED contrast, or you take a lot of photos and want more camera resolution to work with.
  • Look instead at: the Redmi Note 13 4G, which is cheaper and far stronger on exactly those two points.

Redmi Note 13 4G — the screen and camera the others cannot match

If the A07 is the sensible long-term bet, the Redmi Note 13 4G is the phone that feels a class above when you pick it up. Its official price is Rs 42,999 (checked 6 November 2026) — less than the Samsung — yet it brings a 6.67-inch AMOLED panel at 120Hz — a combination of panel type and smoothness that no other phone on this page matches at this money.

The camera hardware follows the same pattern. A 108MP main camera, an 8MP ultrawide and a 2MP macro give you three genuinely different kinds of shot, plus a 16MP front camera. Underneath sits a Snapdragon 685 with a generous 8GB of RAM and 128GB of storage, a 5,000mAh battery and 33W charging.

So why is it not the automatic first pick? Age. This is a 2024 model, and the catalog credits it with two OS updates — a much shorter remaining runway than the Galaxy A07. The hardware will still feel good in two years; the software support simply runs out sooner.

  • Walk past it if: you keep a phone for four or five years and long software support matters more to you than display quality.
  • Look instead at: the Galaxy A07 and its six-year update promise.

Vivo Y21d — the one for people who hate chargers

Some buyers do not care about benchmarks at all. They care that the phone is still alive on the second evening. For them, vivo built the Y21d: a 6,500mAh battery — the joint-largest on this list — paired with 44W charging so that when you do finally plug in, you are not waiting around. Its official price is Rs 43,999 (vivo Pakistan launch price, checked 6 November 2026), and vivo states the phone is PTA approved.

There are two more unusual plus points. Vivo rates the Y21d IP68/IP69 for dust and water resistance, a rating budget phones almost never carry, and the 6.68-inch IPS LCD runs at 90Hz. The rear camera is a single 50MP unit.

The trade sits in the processor. The Unisoc T7225 is entry-level silicon: fine for calls, WhatsApp, video and social apps, but it sets a low ceiling for games.

  • Walk past it if: gaming is a daily habit rather than an occasional distraction.
  • Look instead at: the Galaxy A07 — its Helio G99 is the stronger performer of the two.

Vivo Y29 4G — spending the whole budget, and what it gets you

At Rs 49,999 official price (checked 6 November 2026), the Vivo Y29 4G sits right at the ceiling of this guide, so it has to justify taking every last rupee. Its case rests on a combination: the same 6,500mAh battery and 44W charging as its cheaper sibling, a Snapdragon 685 with 6GB RAM and 128GB storage, and a 6.68-inch screen that pushes the refresh rate up to 120Hz.

One honest catch: that 120Hz panel is IPS LCD, not AMOLED. Scrolling will feel smooth, but blacks and contrast will not match the cheaper Redmi Note 13 4G across the page.

Cameras are a 50MP rear and 8MP front setup — adequate, unremarkable, and clearly not where the money went. The money went into endurance and smoothness.

  • Walk past it if: picture and display quality outrank battery life in your priorities.
  • Look instead at: the Redmi Note 13 4G — it costs Rs 7,000 less and gives you AMOLED.

Oppo A5x — the pick when Rs 50,000 was never really available

Plenty of readers open a "under 50,000" guide with about thirty thousand actually in hand. The Oppo A5x, at Rs 31,999 official price (checked 6 November 2026), is written for them, and it is better than its price suggests. The Helio G100 with 4GB RAM and 64GB storage is a respectable budget processor, the battery is a large 6,000mAh, and 45W charging is — somewhat remarkably — the fastest charging of any phone under Rs 35,000 in this guide.

The 6.67-inch IPS LCD runs at 90Hz, which keeps everyday swiping pleasant. Where the savings show is the camera shelf: a 32MP rear camera and a 5MP front camera are a clear step below the 50MP-and-up hardware elsewhere here.

  • Walk past it if: photos are a big part of how you use a phone.
  • Step up to: the Galaxy A07 if you can stretch the budget; the camera and the update support both improve a lot.

Realme Note 50 — the floor, and knowing it is the floor

At Rs 24,999 official price (checked 6 November 2026), the Realme Note 50 is the cheapest phone we are comfortable putting on this page at all. You get a 6.74-inch IPS LCD at 90Hz, a Unisoc T612 with 4GB RAM and 64GB storage, and a 5,000mAh battery — a workable everyday combination for calls, messaging, browsing and video.

Read the compromises before you buy, though. The rear camera is 13MP and the front camera 5MP, and charging is only 10W, which means long waits at the socket for that big battery. None of that makes it a bad phone at this price; it makes it a phone to buy with your eyes open, because a tight budget is a reason to choose carefully, not a reason to expect more than the money buys.

  • Walk past it if: you can find even a few thousand more — the jump in camera and charging speed is the best-value upgrade on this entire page.
  • Step up to: the Oppo A5x.

What Rs 50,000 still cannot buy in Pakistan

It helps to name the ceiling plainly. Not one phone in this list has 5G — every pick here is a 4G phone, so if next-generation network support is a must-have, this budget has to grow. You will also not find wireless charging, a telephoto camera, or top-tier gaming performance at this level, and only the Redmi Note 13 4G offers an AMOLED screen among the six.

What the budget does buy, if you pick by your own priority — support life, screen, battery, or lowest price — is a phone with no serious weakness for everyday use.

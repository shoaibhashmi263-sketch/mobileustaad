Best Phones Under Rs 75,000 in Pakistan (2026)

Stepping up from Rs 50,000 to Rs 75,000 is the most rewarding jump in the Pakistani market — if you spend it on the right things. This is the band where AMOLED screens, 5G, 256GB storage and genuinely big batteries stop being exceptions. It is also the band where brands pad spec sheets with numbers that do not matter, so every pick below is here for one specific, nameable reason.

Prices are from our catalog, checked 18 November 2026, and labelled official or market. If your budget is actually lower, our best phones under Rs 50,000 guide is the honest starting point.

Tecno Spark 50 — battery first, at the lowest price here

The Spark 50 (Rs 50,999, official Tecno Pakistan price; discounted listings in our catalog start lower) leads with a 7,000mAh battery — the joint-biggest theme of this guide — plus a 6.78-inch 120Hz display, a 50MP main camera, 6GB of RAM and 128GB of storage, running Android 16.

The Helio G81 is a modest processor and charging is 18W, slow for a battery this size. It is an endurance phone, not a performance phone — buy it as one.

  • Walk past it if: you game or multitask heavily.
  • Look instead at: the Realme C85 5G, which pairs a similar battery with a 5G processor and 45W charging.

Samsung Galaxy A16 — buy the next six years

The Galaxy A16 (Rs 54,999, WhatMobile listing; discounted listings from Rs 46,999) is the grown-up choice in this band: a 6.7-inch Super AMOLED display at 90Hz, a 50MP main camera with a 5MP ultrawide, and — the real headline — six OS updates and six years of security updates in our catalog, unmatched here. The Helio G99 with 6GB of RAM and 128GB of storage is adequate rather than quick, and charging is 25W.

If this phone will be yours until 2030, nothing else on this page protects that plan like the A16 does.

  • Walk past it if: you replace phones every two years and would rather have speed now.
  • Look instead at: the Itel Super S26 Ultra.

Itel Super S26 Ultra — the spec sheet that embarrasses bigger brands

Read the Super S26 Ultra's list (Rs 54,999, PriceOye listing) slowly: a 6.78-inch AMOLED display at 144Hz, an in-display fingerprint reader, 8GB of RAM, 256GB of storage, a 6,000mAh battery and a 32MP selfie camera. At this price, that combination is remarkable — itel is buying market share with hardware.

The catch: charging is only 18W for that 6,000mAh battery, and itel's software support record is unpublished. The hardware is excellent; the long-term software story is a question mark.
  • Walk past it if: fast charging or guaranteed updates matter to you.
  • Look instead at: the Galaxy A16 for support, or the Oppo A60 for charging.

Realme C85 5G — 5G and 7,000mAh in one phone

The Realme C85 5G (Rs 54,999, market price) is the only phone here that combines a 7,000mAh battery with a 5G processor — the Dimensity 6300 — and it charges at 45W, so the big battery is not a burden. The 6.8-inch LCD runs at 144Hz, and the main camera is a 50MP unit with a Sony IMX852 sensor.

It carries 4GB of RAM in the catalog variant, which is lean for the price, and the screen is LCD rather than AMOLED. Endurance and connectivity are the purchase; display connoisseurs should look at the itel or Samsung.

  • Walk past it if: screen quality is your top priority.
  • Look instead at: the Itel Super S26 Ultra.

Oppo A60 — the safe, serviceable all-rounder

The Oppo A60 (Rs 54,999) is the least exciting phone here and, for many buyers, the right one: a Snapdragon 680 with a full 8GB of RAM and 128GB of storage, a 50MP main camera, a 5,000mAh battery and 45W charging — the fastest refill among the officially distributed phones on this page. Oppo's service network across Pakistan is a genuine ownership advantage that spec sheets never show.

Its battery is the smallest here and there is no 5G. Buy it for balance and after-sales peace of mind, not for a headline feature.

  • Walk past it if: battery life or 5G leads your list.
  • Look instead at: the Realme C85 5G.

Poco C95 Pro — spend the whole band on size and stamina

At Rs 73,000 (market price), the Poco C95 Pro tops this guide with a 7,500mAh battery, 45W charging and the biggest screen here — a 6.9-inch LCD at 120Hz. For video, reading and long days away from a charger, that combination is the point of the phone. The Helio G91 Ultra and 6GB of RAM are mid-range, and the cameras (50MP main, 8MP front) are serviceable rather than special.

It is a market listing rather than an official launch, so verify PTA approval and warranty with the seller first — our PTA approval guide explains the checks.

  • Walk past it if: you want an official warranty or a compact phone.
  • Look instead at: the Tecno Spark 50 and keep Rs 22,000.

Is the step up from Rs 50,000 worth it?

Yes — when it buys one of three things: an AMOLED screen (A16, S26 Ultra), a much bigger battery (Spark 50, C85 5G, C95 Pro), or years of extra software support (A16 again). If a Rs 75,000 phone offers none of those over a Rs 50,000 one, it is charging for the badge. Compare the exact models side by side on their catalog pages, buy PTA approved from an authorised seller, and keep the warranty card.

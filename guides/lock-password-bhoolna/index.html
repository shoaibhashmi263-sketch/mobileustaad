Forgot the screen lock PIN/password

Phone locked and you can't remember the password? Don't panic — try these official methods first. Any shortcut or third-party tool is risky.

Steps

  1. Verify with your Google account
    Many brands (Samsung, Pixel etc.) offer a 'Forgot password' option after several failed attempts, which unlocks via Google account verification.
  2. Reset the lock via Find My Device
    From another device or browser, open google.com/android/find, log in with your Google account, select your phone and use the 'Secure Device' option — it sets a temporary lock you can use to open the phone.
  3. Samsung users: SmartThings Find
    Samsung account holders can 'Unlock' from smartthingsfind.samsung.com (Remote unlock must already be ON).
  4. Service center: prove ownership
    If nothing works, go to an authorized service center with the phone's purchase receipt / box — they verify ownership and do an official unlock.
  5. Factory reset: last option (data will be lost)
    A factory reset from recovery mode will unlock the phone, but it erases all data — and it will still ask for Google account verification (FRP). Only do this if you have a data backup.

Tips

  • Keep both fingerprint + PIN set up, so if you forget one, the other works.
  • Save your phone PIN in a password manager.

If you have an iPhone

  1. Erase via iCloud
    From any browser, open icloud.com/find, sign in with your Apple ID, select your iPhone and choose 'Erase iPhone'. Once erased, you can set it up again and restore from your iCloud backup — but you will need the same Apple ID and password afterwards (Activation Lock).
  2. No iCloud? Use a computer
    Connect the iPhone to a computer, put it into recovery mode, then restore it with Finder (Mac) or iTunes/Apple Devices (Windows). This also wipes the phone completely, so it only helps if your data is backed up.

Things worth trying before a full reset

  • Stop guessing: After too many wrong attempts the phone locks you out for increasing periods. Random tries only lengthen the lockout, and on iPhone too many wrong codes can disable the phone completely.
  • Check Smart Lock (Android): If you had set up Trusted Places or Trusted Devices earlier, take the phone to that place (for example, home) or near that Bluetooth device — it may unlock without the PIN.
  • Biometrics won't rescue you here: Fingerprint and face unlock stop working right after a restart or a long idle gap — Android and iPhone both demand the PIN or password first in those cases. The PIN is the master key, and there is no official way around it.
  • Ask whoever set the phone up: If a shop or a family member set the phone up for you, ask them — the PIN is often a familiar date or a simple pattern they still remember.

Once you are back in, prevent a repeat

  1. Turn on every unlock option
    Set up fingerprint AND face unlock along with the PIN, and keep the PIN written in a password manager or in a sealed note stored safely at home.
  2. Keep a backup running
    Turn on automatic backup (Google One / Samsung Cloud / iCloud) so that even if a reset is needed next time, your photos, contacts and chats come back.
  3. Save your recovery details
    Make sure the email address and recovery phone number on your Google/Apple/Samsung account are current — account recovery will use exactly those to verify it is really you.

About FRP and Activation Lock: After a factory reset, Android asks for the Google account that was signed in before the reset, and iPhone asks for the original Apple ID. This theft protection is built in by design — nobody, not even a repair shop, can officially remove it without the account details. That is why knowing your own account password matters as much as knowing the PIN.

Warning: Stay away from 'FRP bypass tools' found online — they can be malware and can permanently brick your phone.

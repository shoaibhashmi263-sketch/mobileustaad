Weak signal / calls dropping

Calls cut off mid-way or the signal bars stay stuck at 1–2? Fixes from both the hardware and settings angles are below.

Why signal becomes weak

Your phone is always talking to the nearest tower, and anything between the two weakens that conversation: distance from the tower, thick concrete walls, basements, lifts, metal roofing and even heavy rain. The phone itself can be part of the problem too — an ageing SIM, a metal case, outdated software, or an antenna damaged by a drop. It also helps to know that signal bars are only a rough guide: they measure signal strength, not how busy the tower is. A phone showing full bars can still drop calls on an overloaded tower at peak hours, and two phones showing the same bars can perform quite differently.

Steps

  1. Enable Wi-Fi Calling
    If your home Wi-Fi is good, turn on Settings > SIM > Wi-Fi Calling — calls will go over the internet and never drop.
  2. Select the network manually
    In Settings > SIM > Network Operators, turn OFF 'Choose automatically' and manually select your carrier. Sometimes the phone latches onto a weak tower.
  3. Remove metal cases
    Thick metal or magnetic phone cases block the signal — test with the cover removed.
  4. Carrier settings update
    Install Settings > System > Software Update and carrier-related updates — they include network fixes too.
  5. Replace an old SIM
    SIM cards that are 5–7 years old can cause signal problems. Get a same-number new SIM from a carrier franchise (usually free/cheap).
  6. Report to the helpline
    If everyone in the area has the same problem, complain to the helpline — only the carrier can fix a tower issue.
  7. Restart and re-seat the SIM
    Restart the phone first. If the problem continues, switch it off, take the SIM out, wipe its gold contacts gently with a dry cloth, and insert it firmly — a loosely seated SIM causes random signal loss that comes and goes.
  8. Check the signal strength number
    Settings > About Phone > SIM Status shows signal strength in dBm. Around −50 to −80 dBm is good; −100 dBm or lower is weak. Checking the number in different rooms tells you where in the house reception is genuinely best.
  9. Turn off 5G if it keeps switching
    On the edge of 5G coverage, a phone can drop calls and drain its battery while jumping between 5G and 4G. Set Preferred Network Type to 4G/3G/2G (Auto) for a more stable connection in weak areas.
  10. Test with another SIM
    Put a different carrier's SIM in your phone for a day. If the signal is strong, your carrier's coverage at your location is the problem. If it is still weak with every SIM, your phone itself needs checking.

Weak signal at home only?

If your phone works well outside but loses signal indoors, the building is blocking the signal — very common with concrete construction and metal roofs. Wi-Fi Calling is the best fix when your carrier supports it, because calls and texts then travel over your home internet instead of the tower. Standing near a window for an important call also genuinely helps more than people expect. And if someone in the house makes calls all day for work, ask your carrier about coverage at your exact address before buying a new phone — a new phone on the same network will struggle in the same rooms.

When to get the phone checked

Visit a service centre if the signal problem began right after a drop or a repair, if the phone shows 'No Service' with different SIMs, or if it loses the network completely several times a day while other phones nearby stay connected. These symptoms point to an antenna or board fault rather than a coverage issue. Before you go, note when the problem happens and test with another SIM — that information helps the technician find the fault much faster.

Protect your reception

  • Choose slim, non-metal cases — bulky metal and magnetic covers weaken the signal.
  • Replace a SIM that is many years old; worn contacts make the connection unreliable.
  • Keep the phone software updated — modem fixes ride along with system updates.
  • After any screen or body repair, test calls before leaving the shop; antenna cables are easy to disturb during a repair.

Tips

  • Everyone's signal is weak in basements and parking lots — that's not the phone's fault.
  • On dual SIM, keep the call SIM on 2G/3G and the data SIM on 4G/5G for the best of both.

Phone is lost — locate it

Forgot your phone somewhere or was it stolen? Acting fast is the biggest fix. Being prepared (Find My Device ON) is a must.

Steps

  1. Open Find My Device
    From another phone or computer, log in with your Google account at google.com/android/find. The phone's current location will show on the map (if the internet is ON).
  2. Ring it
    If the phone is somewhere in the house or office, tap 'Play Sound' — the phone will ring at full volume for 5 minutes even on silent.
  3. Secure Device (lock it)
    Use 'Secure Device' to lock the phone and set a contact number/message on the lock screen so whoever finds it can reach you.
  4. Get the SIM blocked
    If you suspect the phone won't come back, immediately get the SIM temporarily blocked on your carrier's helpline — stay safe from misuse. The PTA helpline can also block the IMEI.
  5. Erase data: last option
    Doing 'Erase Device' from Google wipes all data on the phone. Do this when hope of getting it back is gone — after erasing, the phone can't be located.
  6. File a report
    In case of theft, definitely file a police report and a PTA complaint (with the IMEI).

Tips

  • Keep the phone's IMEI number (dial *#06#) safe on the box/receipt — that's the phone's identity.
  • For the future: keep 'Find My Device' and location history ON in Settings.

If the lost phone is an iPhone

  1. Use Apple's Find My
    Open icloud.com/find in any browser or the Find My app on another Apple device, and sign in with the same Apple ID. You can see the location, play a sound, or turn on 'Lost Mode' to lock the phone and show your contact number on its screen.
  2. Check the last known location
    If the battery died, Find My shows the last location before it switched off. Many newer iPhones can also be located for some hours after powering off — always check before assuming the phone is gone for good.

If the phone is off, offline, or the battery is dead

  • Google Maps Timeline: If Location History was on, open Google Maps > Your Timeline on the account signed into the phone — the places it visited that day often tell you exactly where it was left.
  • Call and message it first: Before assuming theft, call from another phone and send an SMS or WhatsApp message with your contact number. Honest finders usually answer within a few hours.
  • Know what the carrier can do: Your mobile company can confirm whether the SIM is still active and, on a police request, help further. Only the police and the PTA can act on IMEI tracking — any website or shop claiming to 'track any IMEI for a fee' is a scam.

Protect your accounts immediately

  1. Change your key passwords
    From a safe device, change the passwords of your Google or Apple account, your email, and your banking apps, and choose 'sign out of all other sessions' wherever that option exists.
  2. Secure your mobile wallets and banking
    Call your bank and SIM helpline to block mobile banking and wallet services linked to that number, until you get a replacement SIM issued on the same number.
  3. Warn your contacts
    Tell family and close friends the phone is lost, so nobody trusts a strange message or a money request that appears to come from your number.

Set this up today, before it happens

  • Turn on Find My Device / Find My and Location, and test it once from a browser so you already know it works.
  • Keep a photo of the phone's box (with the IMEI printed on it) in your email or cloud storage — you will need the IMEI for the police report and for PTA blocking.
  • Add a lock-screen message with an alternate contact number, so a finder can reach you without unlocking the phone.

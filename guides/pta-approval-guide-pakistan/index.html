PTA Approval Guide: How to Check and Register a Phone in Pakistan

"PTA approved" is a phrase every phone buyer in Pakistan hears, usually at the moment a seller is asking for more money. This guide explains what it actually means, how to check a phone yourself for free, and why some registration routes apply to some people and not to others.

Start with the IMEI

Every phone carries a 15-digit IMEI — its identity number on mobile networks. Dial *#06# and it appears on screen. A dual-SIM phone has two IMEIs, and some phones add a separate eSIM IMEI as well. That detail matters more than most buyers realise: each IMEI must be approved separately, so approving the first one tells you nothing about the second.

The free check anyone can do

Text the IMEI to 8484. The reply will tell you whether the device is compliant — worded as "Compliant (PTA Approved/Registered)" — non-compliant, or blocked. There is no charge for finding out, and no account to create.

If you prefer a screen to a text message, the same system is reachable other ways: the Device Verification System at dirbs.pta.gov.pk, the USSD menu on *8484#, and the official DIRBS app. Registration itself is handled through the portal at dirbs.pta.gov.pk/drs, which is accessible from inside Pakistan.

What DIRBS is, in plain terms

DIRBS stands for Device Identification, Registration and Blocking System, and it is run by the PTA. It is the register that decides whether a particular device may use Pakistani mobile networks. A phone that is not approved does not become useless — it still works on Wi-Fi — but once it is blocked, its SIM stops working on Pakistani networks. For most people, that distinction is the whole purchase.

There is some breathing room for a phone brought in from abroad. An imported phone first used with a Pakistani SIM gets a grace period of 60 days before it is blocked if it remains unregistered. Useful for a short visit; not a loophole for permanent use.

Who sets the tax — and why this guide quotes no figure

Registration involves a tax, and the tax is set by the FBR (Federal Board of Revenue), not by the PTA. It is worked out from the device's assessed value and model in the FBR's valuation tables, which is not necessarily the same as the price you happened to pay for it.

Because the amount depends on the specific device and the current valuation table, this guide deliberately does not quote tax figures. Numbers copied from a forum or a shop's noticeboard may belong to a different model or an older table. Check your exact device through the official DIRBS registration portal instead.

CNIC registration and passport registration

Registration falls into two broad categories. Pakistani citizens register on a CNIC. Foreign nationals and overseas Pakistanis who are visiting register on a passport. The category is not a formality — it affects the tax treatment — so travellers should register under the category that matches their actual status rather than whichever route a shop suggests.

What "PTA approved" should mean at the counter

Used properly, the phrase means the IMEI is registered in DIRBS and its tax has been paid, so the phone works on all Pakistani networks permanently. Phones sold officially through brand stores and authorised dealers are sold already approved, which is why buying through those channels removes this entire question. The uncertainty lives in imported, market-channel and second-hand purchases — exactly where the 8484 check earns its keep.

Red flags worth walking away from

  • A price far below the normal market price for that model. Genuine bargains exist, but a gap that large usually has an explanation, and the explanation is rarely in your favour.
  • "CPID" or patched phones — devices whose IMEI has been tampered with so they imitate another device. They can be blocked later, and selling one as approved is illegal.
  • A seller who discourages you from sending the IMEI to 8484 while they watch. An honest seller has nothing to lose from a free check.
  • The IMEI printed on the box does not match the one shown by *#06#.
  • Only one of the two IMEIs on a dual-SIM phone turns out to be approved.
  • A "non-PTA" phone sold for Wi-Fi-only use being described, in the same conversation, as approved.

Check before you pay: the routine

  1. Ask for the phone, dial *#06#, and note every IMEI shown — two on a dual-SIM model.
  2. Compare those numbers with the IMEI printed on the box.
  3. Send each IMEI by SMS to 8484 and read the reply for every one of them, not just the first.
  4. Confirm the reply says the device is compliant before any money changes hands.
  5. If the phone is unregistered and you still want it, check the exact device through the DIRBS registration portal and register under the category — CNIC or passport — that matches your status.

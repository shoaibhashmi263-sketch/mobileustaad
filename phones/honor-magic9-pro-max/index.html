Overview

Few phones stack specifications as aggressively as this one. Its expected price for Pakistan is Rs 269,100, worked out from the China launch price of CNY 6,499 for 12GB/256GB, and since Honor has not launched it here, that figure describes an import scenario rather than a shop shelf in Karachi or Lahore. The 6.8-inch LTPO OLED reaches extraordinary brightness figures — 2,000 nits in high-brightness mode and a claimed 10,000-nit peak — with Dolby Vision, HDR Vivid, 4,320Hz PWM dimming and NanoCrystal Shield protection. Underneath, the 2nm Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 with Honor's Phantom Engine 3.0 earns an exceptional gaming rating, handling 120Hz play at maximum settings. Photography hardware is unusually deep: a 200MP wide camera with OIS and gimbal-level stabilisation, a 200MP periscope telephoto with OIS and 3.5x optical zoom, and a 50MP ultrawide spanning 122 degrees, all ARRI co-branded, while the 55MP front camera shoots in a square format and works with 3D face recognition. An 8,800mAh battery supports 100W wired and 80W wireless charging. The record credits it with up to seven major OS updates and seven years of security patches on Android 17 and MagicOS 11, though the phone itself weighs a hefty 233g. Choose it for camera reach, screen brightness and endurance. Its size, weight and import-only status will rule it out for many buyers.

Gaming review

The Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 is the current top Qualcomm chip — high-refresh 120Hz gaming at max settings is no problem, backed by 12-16GB LPDDR5X RAM and Honor's Phantom Engine 3.0.

Our Verdict

The Honor Magic9 Pro Max is a flagship-grade phone that leads with 120Hz LTPO OLED (1320x2868, 1B colors, Dolby Vision, HDR Vivid, 4320Hz PWM, 2000 nits HBM, 10,000 nits peak, NanoCrystal Shield, anti-reflective coating) panel keeps scrolling and animations fluid. It packs the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 (SM8975, 2nm; octa-core 2x5.01GHz Prime + 3x4.03GHz + 3x3.74GHz, Adreno 850) chipset and a 8,800mAh battery with 100W charging. On the software side it runs Android 17 (MagicOS 11), and Honor promises 7 years of Android updates. It makes a strong case for itself in this price band — though buyers should weigh this first: the 3.5mm headphone jack is missing.

Overview

Samsung's Galaxy A08 LTE arrived in Pakistan on 19 November 2026 as a budget 4G option, with an official price of Rs 55,999 for the 4GB RAM and 64GB version; stepping up costs Rs 67,999 for 4GB/128GB and Rs 78,999 for 6GB/128GB. Its 6.7-inch PLS LCD runs at HD+ resolution (720x1600) with a 90Hz refresh rate, so scrolling feels smoother than basic panels while the resolution keeps pixel density modest on a screen this large. A side-mounted fingerprint reader, IP64 rating, 3.5mm headphone jack and microSD support up to 2TB round out a practical hardware list, although NFC is absent.

Power comes from the MediaTek Helio G99+ built on a 6nm process, and Samsung rates gaming as good: popular titles such as PUBG Mobile and Free Fire run smoothly at balanced settings. Photography rests on a 50MP main camera with autofocus but no optical stabilisation, helped only by a 2MP depth sensor, plus an 8MP front camera, so expect ordinary daylight photos and portrait assistance rather than zoom or wide-angle flexibility. The 6,000mAh battery pairs with 25W charging, though no charger sits inside the box.

Software is a standout, because the phone ships with Android 17 and One UI 9 and Samsung promises six generations of OS upgrades alongside six years of security updates, until 30 September 2032, with Knox Vault included. Choose it for long software support, expandable storage and dependable everyday use. Skip it if 5G, NFC payments or stronger cameras matter more to you.

Our Verdict

With the Samsung Galaxy A08 LTE, Samsung is aiming at buyers who want large 6.7-inch PLS LCD (HD+ 720x1600) display without paying flagship money. It packs the MediaTek Helio G99+ (6nm) chipset and a 6,000mAh battery with 25W charging. Software is Android 17 (One UI 9), backed by a promise of 6 years of Android updates from Samsung. It is one of the more rounded options in its bracket — if you can overlook one thing: no 5g support — 4g only.

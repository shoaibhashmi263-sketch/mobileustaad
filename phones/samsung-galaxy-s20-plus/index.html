Overview

Samsung's Galaxy S20+ is now a discontinued older flagship, and the Rs 185,000 figure in the record is its last known official price via WhatMobile, not a current launch price. Its 6.7-inch Dynamic AMOLED 2X screen offers a sharp 1440 x 3200 resolution and 120Hz refresh, which still reads as a high-quality panel for video and browsing, even if the phone originally shipped on Android 10 with One UI 2.1.

Inside, the Exynos 990 handles high-frame-rate gameplay smoothly on that display, helped by the larger chassis and 4,500mAh battery giving more stamina than the base S20 for longer sessions. The record is candid about the weakness too: thermal throttling under extended load, so competitive players may need a frame-rate cap or cooling. Software support in the record is listed as three years of OS updates and four years of security updates, a window that reflects its 2020 launch timing.

The rear cameras are listed as 64MP, 12MP, 12MP and a 0.3MP ToF sensor, with a 10MP camera in front. The record does not label those lenses as telephoto or ultrawide, so do not buy expecting the clearly defined zoom system found on newer rivals. Charging is 25W, modest by today's standards, and there is no 5G on this variant and no headphone jack. It may suit someone finding a well-kept unit for its screen and Samsung software; anyone wanting 5G, long remaining support or fast charging should move on.

Gaming review

The larger display and 4500mAh battery give the S20+ more stamina for long gaming sessions than the base S20, and the Exynos 990 drives high-frame-rate gameplay smoothly on the 120Hz panel. Thermal throttling under extended load is the main weakness, so competitive players may want a cooler or frame-rate cap.

Our Verdict

Positioned as a flagship-grade phone, the Samsung Galaxy S20+ puts smooth 120Hz Dynamic AMOLED 2X, 120Hz display that feels fast in everyday use at the top of its pitch. It packs the Exynos 990 (7 nm+) chipset and a 4,500mAh battery with 25W charging. It earns a recommendation for most buyers in its class — the one thing to accept first is this: no headphone jack — wireless or usb-c audio only.

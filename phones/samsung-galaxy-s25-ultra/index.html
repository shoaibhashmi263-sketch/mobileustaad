Overview

Samsung's 2025 Ultra opens at an official price of Rs 332,999, with PriceOye showing a market price of Rs 354,999 for the 12GB and 256GB configuration. A 6.9-inch Dynamic LTPO AMOLED 2X display delivers QHD+ resolution at 1440x3120 and refreshes at 120Hz, giving video and games a large, sharp stage. Driving everything is the Snapdragon 8 Elite on a 3nm process, with 12GB of RAM and 256GB of storage.

According to the gaming notes, this chip pushes 120fps in demanding titles such as PUBG Mobile and Genshin Impact at maximum settings, and vapor-chamber cooling keeps throttling in check during sustained play, earning it a mention as arguably the best Android gaming phone of 2025. Four rear cameras are listed at 200MP, 50MP, 50MP and 10MP, a spread that suggests serious flexibility across shooting distances, while selfies rely on a 12MP unit. The 5,000mAh battery charges at 45W, the fingerprint reader is ultrasonic and in-display, and connectivity covers 5G, NFC, Bluetooth 5.4 and USB Type-C 3.2.

It arrived with Android 15 and One UI 7; the record leaves OS and security update years unpublished, so treat support length as unconfirmed here. Choose it for the largest Samsung screen in this batch paired with the fastest listed Android performance. Steer clear if you find big handsets unwieldy or if 45W charging feels slow for the money.

Our analysis: the complete flagship, priced like one — warranty is where quotes really differ

The Galaxy S25 Ultra does not specialise; it accumulates. A Snapdragon 8 Elite processor, 12GB of RAM with 256GB of storage, a 6.9-inch QHD+ Dynamic LTPO AMOLED 2X display at 120Hz, an ultrasonic fingerprint reader and the S Pen — the stylus that remains this line's defining difference from every slab flagship rival — form the base. The camera array then adds range rather than a single trick: a 200MP main camera, a 50MP ultrawide, a 50MP periscope telephoto and a 10MP telephoto. Battery capacity is 5,000mAh with 45W charging, figures that look ordinary on paper next to larger Chinese-flagship cells, and heavy buyers should factor that in rather than discover it.

What that accumulation buys is the absence of an obvious weak department. There is no specification here that forces a particular kind of user to walk away, which is precisely what a flagship at this level is supposed to achieve — and precisely why the decision usually comes down to price and warranty rather than features.

Two very different flagship arguments

The Vivo X200 Pro is the value challenge: Rs 329,999 official, 16GB of RAM with 512GB of storage, a Dimensity 9400, a 200MP periscope telephoto and a 6,000mAh battery with 90W charging — roughly Rs 85,000 less than Samsung's official figure, with more memory and storage. The iPhone 17 Pro Max, at Rs 564,999, is not really a specification rival at all; at roughly Rs 150,000 more, with its 48MP triple camera system, it is the ecosystem choice — bought for iOS, resale behaviour and an existing Apple setup, not for winning a feature count. The S25 Ultra sits between those arguments: dearer than the Vivo, cheaper than the iPhone, and the only one of the three offering the S Pen.

Our Verdict

The Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra is a flagship-grade phone that leads with fast 120Hz Dynamic LTPO AMOLED 2X screen, a clear step above 60Hz panels. Headline hardware: the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite (3 nm) chipset and a 5,000mAh battery with 45W charging. Shortlist it if the spec mix suits you — if you can overlook one thing: no headphone jack — wireless or usb-c audio only.

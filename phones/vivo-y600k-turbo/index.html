Overview

Vivo's Y600k Turbo leads with endurance and screen brightness, and its Rs 115,900 tag is an expected price converted from the Chinese launch figure of CNY 2,799, since Pakistan has no official launch yet. The 6.83-inch AMOLED panel offers 1.5K resolution at 1260x2800, a 120Hz refresh rate, 5,000 nits peak brightness, 4320Hz PWM dimming and HDR support, which is an unusually rich display specification for the Y series. Stereo speakers, an IR blaster, NFC and an in-display optical fingerprint reader add further polish.

Qualcomm's Snapdragon 7s Gen 4, a 4nm chip paired with a 7K vapor chamber cooling system, earns a very good gaming rating, holding stable frame rates in popular titles at high settings. Cameras are less ambitious: a 50MP main sensor with a 2MP depth helper records 4K video with gyro-EIS, but no ultrawide or telephoto is listed, and the front camera is only 8MP, which limits selfie detail. The battery is the centrepiece at 9,020mAh typical capacity, or 8,820mAh rated, with 90W wired charging plus reverse wired and bypass charging.

OriginOS 6 based on Android 16 runs the show, although Vivo has not published an update policy for this phone and bloatware is rated medium. It makes sense for heavy users who stream, play and travel away from chargers. Selfie-focused buyers and anyone needing an official Pakistani launch should hesitate.

Gaming review

The Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 with a 7K vapor chamber handles popular titles at high settings with stable frame rates; the 120Hz 1.5K AMOLED and the big battery make it a strong mid-range gaming pick.

Our Verdict

With the Vivo Y600k Turbo, Vivo is aiming at buyers who want 120Hz 1.5K AMOLED (1260x2800, 120Hz, 5000 nits peak, 4320Hz PWM dimming, 10-bit, HDR, 450ppi) panel keeps scrolling and animations fluid without paying flagship money. Headline hardware: the Qualcomm Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 (4nm; octa-core 1x2.7 GHz Cortex-A720 + 3x2.4 GHz Cortex-A720 + 4x1.8 GHz Cortex-A520, Adreno 810; 7K VC cooling) chipset and a 9,020mAh battery with 90W charging. It runs Android 16 (OriginOS 6) out of the box. It earns a recommendation for most buyers in its class — if you can overlook one thing: no 3.5mm headphone jack.
